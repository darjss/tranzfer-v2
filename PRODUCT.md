# Tranzfer — Product & Pricing

## Product

Tranzfer is a large-file delivery tool for creative work.

It is built for the annoying real-world case: hundreds of gigabytes, imperfect Wi‑Fi, sleeping laptops, refreshed tabs, long uploads, and recipients who just need one link that works.

It is **not permanent cloud storage**, a drive replacement, or a cheap WeTransfer clone.

## Unique selling point

**Huge file transfers that recover instead of restart.**

Tranzfer is designed around 100–500+ GB projects where restarting an interrupted upload can waste hours.

The product should preserve completed work, verify what already arrived, and continue from what is missing.

That is the reason to choose Tranzfer.

## Core philosophy

**Keep the work already done.**

Interruptions are normal, not exceptional.

- Resume before restart.
- Verify before re-uploading.
- Keep progress honest.
- Sender uploads; recipient gets one simple link.
- Files expire after delivery instead of becoming permanent cloud storage.
- Reliability and clarity beat feature count.

A user should trust Tranzfer with a 300 GB folder because it feels calmer and more dependable than the alternatives.

## Product advantages

**Built for huge deliveries.** Hundreds of gigabytes are a normal use case, not an edge case.

**Recovery-first.** Bad Wi‑Fi, sleep, refreshes, and expired authorization should not throw away hours of progress.

**Simple delivery.** Recipients should not need an account. One link should be enough.

**Temporary by design.** Short retention keeps the product focused on delivery and keeps costs sane.

**Creative-tool taste.** Landing page, dashboard, upload progress, and download pages should feel like one authored product rather than generic SaaS UI.

**Honest state.** Never imply recovery or completion that has not actually happened.

## Product principles

- Reliability before features.
- One obvious path to send and receive.
- No enterprise ceremony for consumer problems.
- Do not hide resumability behind expensive tiers.
- Do not become Dropbox by accident.
- The dashboard deserves the same design quality as the landing page.
- Prefer real product screenshots and authentic creative-work imagery over obvious AI-generated lifestyle images.
- Ship the smallest complete workflow before adding adjacent features.

## How capacity works

Capacity is **active transfer space**, not permanent storage and not a monthly bandwidth quota.

When a delivery expires or is deleted, that space becomes available again. A user may send more than their plan size over a month as long as active deliveries stay within the limit.

## Pricing principles

- Do not compete on being the cheapest.
- The entry tier should be easy to justify against established tools.
- Starter gets the core product; higher tiers mainly buy capacity and retention.
- Avoid promising team, branding, request-link, or enterprise features before they exist.

## Cost guardrail

R2 storage is roughly **$0.015/GB-month**.

Worst case if a user continuously fills their allocation:

- 300 GB ≈ **$4.50/month**
- 1 TB ≈ **$15/month**
- 3 TB ≈ **$45/month**

The model assumes normal delivery behavior: upload, download, then expire or delete. Beta usage should validate average active GB and retention before pricing is treated as final.

## Beta

Keep beta free and small enough to observe closely.

Measure:

- average active GB
- average retention time
- repeat usage
- interrupted-upload recovery
- how often users hit capacity
- which paid tier they naturally fit into

The beta is successful when people trust Tranzfer with real large projects and come back to use it again.
