We make Tranzfer, so read our ranking with that in mind. Drive is a good place to keep files, and for some sends it's the right tool. Every fact about another service links to the page it came from.

## Why do people look for a Google Drive alternative?

Most people end up in Drive by accident. Personal Gmail allows [25 MB of attachments](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6584) and swaps anything bigger for a Drive link. That works for a deck. It gets awkward at 200 GB.

**Storage fills up.** A free Google account has [15 GB](https://support.google.com/googleone/answer/9312312), shared across Gmail, Drive and Photos. Every file you send stays in your Drive and counts against that space until you delete it. A 300 GB shoot needs a storage plan big enough to hold it, plus whatever you already keep there.

**There's a daily cap.** Google's [Workspace limits page](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/drive/storage-and-upload-limits-for-google-workspace) says "Each user can upload and copy 750 GB to Drive within 24 hours" and that "Only the first file that breaks the limit completes uploading." Google's [page for personal accounts](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/2565956) says there's a daily upload limit but gives no number.

**A broken browser upload is a mystery.** Google's [developer docs](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/manage-uploads) describe resumable uploads for apps built on the Drive API. What the Drive website does after you close the tab or lose the connection halfway through isn't written down anywhere we could find. Google's [sync help](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/2565956) says Drive for desktop "will automatically retry later", and files it can't sync can land in a Lost and Found folder.

**Popular links get throttled.** Share one file with a lot of people and Google may restrict it for heavy downloads. Google says that [typically resets within 24 hours](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/2423534).

**Links don't end.** "Anyone who has the link can use your file, without signing in", as Google's [sharing page](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/2494822) puts it. Expiry dates exist only for eligible work and school accounts. A client link from last year still works unless you remember to turn it off.

## Which one should you pick?

- **You send 50 GB to 3 TB and want it gone afterwards.** Tranzfer. Files are deleted when the link ends, so they never sit in your storage. You sign in with Google to send, and your recipient doesn't need an account.
- **You want a desktop app and send irregularly.** MASV, at $0.25 per GB after 15 GB free each month.
- **You want files kept for good and don't mind an app.** Filemail Pro.
- **You send a few gigabytes to someone who expects a familiar link.** WeTransfer.

Check how long your upload will run before you start it. The [upload time calculator](/tools/upload-time-calculator) does the math. A 300 GB folder on a 100 Mbps line takes 6 h 40 min at full speed, and about 8 h 20 min at a realistic 80%.

## When is Google Drive still the better pick?

- The files should stay. Drive is storage, and Tranzfer isn't. Our links last 14 days at most, then the files are deleted.
- You're sharing a working folder, not a delivery. People can comment on Docs, edit Sheets and add files to a shared folder. Tranzfer has no request links and no comments.
- Your recipient lives in Google already, and you already pay for the storage. For a 10 GB send, that's free and good enough.
- You need a single file bigger than 3 TB. Drive takes [up to 5 TB](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/37603). Tranzfer's ceiling is 3 TB live at once.
