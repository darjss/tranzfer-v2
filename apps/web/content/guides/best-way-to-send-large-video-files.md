We make Tranzfer, and it's first on this list for the job we built it for. It isn't first for every job, and the list says where something else wins. Facts about other services come from their own pages, checked 9 October 2026, and each is linked.

## Which tool is best for which video job?

1. **[Tranzfer](/features/send-large-files), for whole shoots from a browser.** Hundreds of gigabytes, folders kept, a flat monthly price. If the Wi-Fi drops it retries, and after a crash or closed tab you pick the same files and only the missing parts upload. No desktop app, links last 14 days at most.
2. **MASV, if you want a desktop app and pay per use.** $0.25 per GB after 15 GB free each month, portals for collecting files from clients, and a desktop app that recovers from reboots. In the browser, a closed tab means starting over.
3. **Frame.io, for review and approval.** Frame-accurate comments, versions and share links that work without an account. It's storage priced per member, so files stay until you delete them. More on the [Frame.io alternatives](/alternatives/frame-io) page.
4. **Filemail, for files that should stay up for good.** Pro keeps files permanently and Business takes any size. Resume is a desktop app feature.
5. **WeTransfer, for short exports to someone who expects it.** Everyone knows the link. Free and Starter cap what you send in 30 days, and their links last 3 days.
6. **Dropbox Transfer or Google Drive, if your team already pays for it.** Up to 100 or 250 GB a transfer on Dropbox business plans, files up to 5 TB on Drive. Both are storage first, and neither documents what a browser upload does after a closed tab.

## What matters most when sending video?

**Surviving an interruption.** A 300 GB shoot on a 50 Mbps upload runs for 13 hours 20 minutes at full speed. Something will happen in 13 hours. Check what each tool does when it does, in the browser and not just in a desktop app. Most pages don't say, which is why the table above has so many "not stated" cells.

**Sending the folder as it is.** Cameras write card structures, sidecars and metadata that editing apps read. A tool that keeps folders saves your editor from rebuilding them. Our [guide to transferring raw footage](/guides/send-large-video-files-to-an-editor) has the prep steps.

**What the recipient has to do.** None of the tools above ask the recipient to sign up to download. On Tranzfer, desktop Chrome and Edge save a whole delivery into one folder in one go; other browsers download file by file.

**How the bill grows.** Per-GB pricing is cheap in a quiet month and expensive in a busy one. Per-seat storage like Frame.io's grows with the team. A flat plan like ours stays put but counts what's live at once. The [price calculator](/pricing) runs your own numbers.

## When is shipping a drive better?

When the upload would take days. At 20 Mbps, 2 TB takes over 9 days at full speed. A drive in the post gets there in one or two, and for multi-terabyte projects on slow lines that's still the right answer.
